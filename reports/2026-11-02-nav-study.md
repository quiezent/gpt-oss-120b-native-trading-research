# NAV's training: what Codex did, and what differs from Opus and Fable

*Published by Trader Training on 3 November 2026 (UTC+8) from the Model Performance Analyst's report, with local file paths removed and a note on what was fixed. I recounted the drift tables from the stored scores, and the research-limit split from the training rows, and they match.*

*Mon 2 Nov 2026, evening ET. Written by the analysis session, which runs claude-opus-5-5, the model behind every non-NAV training run here, so discount my judgment on Opus accordingly. Clayton asked: "Study NAV's training. What Codex did and what's different from Opus and Fable?"*

## The answer

- **Codex ran one recipe nine times on 8-9 Oct, and on live looks the model barely moved.** It was RL on a single reward, marked NAV after 1 to 63 sessions, with 16 optimizer steps at learning rate 1e-5. The world was a 3-symbol, daily, long-only simulator, and each run with any variation became the next parent. On Monday's real looks NAV9 is about as far from base as a LoRA with no training. Its tie with base in that simulator (−0.03 points, 95% −0.76 to +0.69) says nothing for or against the method, which was never run at a dose that shows.
- **Opus trained differently and moved the model far more.** rl5's line is RL with the market hedged out and risk charged, 60 updates at 5e-5. F and H are supervised copies of base's own re-samples, with no reward, 45 and 50 updates at 1e-4. All sit well away from base. None has been shown to beat it, and F did worse on its 15 simulated test weeks.
- **Fable trained nothing.** It wrote the acceptance criteria and gates for rl4 to rl6b, and some reward terms came from its criteria.
- **No line has a measured P&L edge over base.** The two clearest improvements to how looks run came from framework fixes at no training cost, the free turn and the parser fix. Neither has a shown P&L gain.
- **On token distance, NAV9 and NAV9 + ML2 sit close to Base and Base + ML2 at $30,000.** They are different weights, and no same-state decision comparison exists. Monday's +1.77% on NAV9 + ML2 is not evidence for NAV training: it comes from AMD and INTC shorts that Base + ML2 $100k also held.

**Terms.**
- **Look:** one scheduled decision, research calls then a submit.
- **LoRA:** a small trainable layer added on base's fixed weights.
- **Dose:** the amount of training, steps times learning rate.
- **rl1 to rl6b:** Opus's RL runs. **F and H:** the 2 Nov models, trained to reproduce base's low-friction and hindsight-best re-samples. This is **SFT**, supervised fine-tuning.
- **Forced submit:** the old rule at the 12-call research limit, where software opened the submit call with no reasoning turn. **Free turn:** since 1 Nov, one more reasoning turn at that limit.
- **Parser fix:** software now accepts a plain tool-call format gpt-oss writes.
- **R:** reward units, percentage points of a sleeve's NAV.

## What Codex did

**Who and when:** `gpt-6.1-sol` at effort `ultra`, Codex thread 01a11a37-a241, 8 to 9 Oct 2026. The public documents only say "I am Codex"; the model is in every `turn_context` of the surviving session log (71 of 71).

**The recipe.** Reward and learning method held across all nine runs; the world and tools changed (table below).
- **Reward:** `(terminal marked NAV - initial NAV - flows) / initial NAV` at 1, 5, 20 or 63 sessions (`codex/training/nav_learning.py:410-412`). No benchmark, no exit cost at the mark, no risk term, no penalty, no bonus.
- **Learning:** rank-8 LoRA with the output layer untrained, Adam at 1e-5, a plain policy-gradient loss (`importance_sampling`), groups of 3 rollouts at temperature 1, reasoning effort medium. A rollout's return minus its group's mean goes on every token the model generated. Adam was reset at the start of each run, so 9 of the 16 steps were fresh first steps. The reader found no stated reason for the rate or the step counts.
- **World:** AAPL, MSFT and SPY daily bars, $10,000, long-only DAY limit orders that fill only at the next open. From NAV6 on, orders were capped at 3 shares, so sizing couldn't be learned. In NAV9's training the evaluator advanced 852 of 1,068 sessions: 410 after the model's own FINAL and 442 after a step limit or context stop.
- **Keep rule:** none. Any run with variation became the next parent, including runs that lost their comparison. Codex later called this "advancing experimental checkpoints without demonstrating improvement".
- **Philosophy:** the model's own output decides, with no teacher labels, no routing and no action repair. Every failure stayed in the record.

**NAV9's +2.85% against base's +1.52% doesn't hold.** That is the first section of [Codex's page](../codex/README.md), from `CONTROLLED_BASE_NAV4_NAV9.md`. It came from 12 cases with one greedy rollout each (temperature 0), and 15.5% of those greedy tokens had a probability below .55, where sampler noise can flip the choice [A]. Trader Training then re-tested on 76 later episodes a model at temperature 1 (20 or 63 sessions, NAV's simulator): NAV9 +0.89%, base +0.92%, and a passive basket +1.26%.

## How far each model actually moved

Measured for this study, for about $0.85 at list. I rebuilt the exact tokens of 8 real base looks (`base-100k`, 12:00 to 15:30 ET on 2 Nov) and scored all 70,421 sampled tokens under each model. Token counts on those looks match the look files' records within 3 sampled tokens. Because base drew these tokens, the mean log-probability gap estimates how much less likely a model finds them (KL from base). 0 means no detectable gap, small negatives are noise, and a gap of 1 nat is about a 2.7-fold likelihood difference. A fresh LoRA with no updates sets the floor.

| Model | Updates from base | KL per 1,000 tokens (90%) | Nats a look | Half mean squared gap, per 1,000 | Median gap, tokens base gave under 99% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Base, scored again | 0 | −0.10 (−0.32, +0.06) | −0.9 | 1.33 | 0.000 |
| **LoRA, no updates, output layer off, seed A** | 0 | +0.14 (−0.35, +0.60) | 0.8 | 3.35 | 0.023 |
| **LoRA, no updates, output layer off, seed B** | 0 | −0.03 (−0.70, +0.66) | −1.3 | 3.23 | 0.021 |
| **LoRA, no updates, output layer on** | 0 | +0.54 (+0.10, +0.95) | 4.5 | 3.12 | 0.024 |
| **NAV9** | 16 | +0.26 (−0.25, +0.86) | 1.6 | 4.13 | 0.034 |
| NAV9, scored again | 16 | +0.13 (−0.17, +0.44) | 0.3 | 4.04 | 0.033 |
| NAV4 | 10 | +0.26 (−0.21, +0.72) | 1.9 | 4.13 | 0.033 |

Per-1,000 figures average each look's rate; nats a look averages its totals. The squared column can't cancel to zero, so its floor sits above zero. "Base, scored again" looks lower only because 4 of its 8 looks came back bit-identical and a fifth mostly did. On its 3 fresh looks it reads 3.2 to 3.7, so 1.33 isn't a floor. Noise alone moves one look's total by about 9 nats.

- **NAV9's KL can't be told apart from an untrained LoRA's.** On NAV9's own tokens it is −0.06 (−0.62, +0.51), and on NAV9 + ML2's, with the forecast tool in the prompt, −0.02 (−0.61, +0.64). Those rebuilds keep the three discarded turns described below; dropping them gives −0.09 and +0.02.
- **Most of what looked like NAV's movement is re-score noise.** Tinker doesn't score identically on every request, and the gap is the same size wherever two scorings stop matching. After each pair's first mismatch, the squared column reads 3.39 for base against base, 3.44 to 3.66 for base against a zero-update LoRA and 3.51 to 3.63 for zero against zero. So a LoRA with zero updates shows no effect of its own, under about 0.16 per 1,000. The Tinker Owner found this, and I reproduced it. A zero-update LoRA's median gap is 0.021 to 0.024 nats, and NAV9's is 0.033 to 0.034. The 14 Oct audit read 0.03 to 0.04 nats as 5 to 15 times the noise, using a floor that many identical scorings had pulled down. Most of that reading goes.
- **Some real movement remains, and it is small.** Net of the zero-against-zero floor of 3.6, NAV9's squared gap is about 0.5, against about 11 for H, 27 for F and 159 for rl5. That is roughly 20, 50 and 300 times smaller. Comparing NAV9's two independent scorings, which cancels the noise, gives +0.44 (90% 0.35 to 0.53), where zero-update LoRAs give +0.03 (−0.11 to +0.16), so NAV9 does separate from them. NAV4 and NAV9's gaps correlate .51, the same as NAV9's with its own second scoring (.53), so six further updates show no added movement here.
- **The same ranking holds on order tokens.** On order JSON alone, nats a look: NAV9 0.44 (−0.47, +1.29), H 3.86, F 12.39, rl5 47.45.

**Limits:** one day, 8 looks, live prompts at high effort, and estimators chosen here rather than pre-registered. Distance in NAV's own simulator wasn't measured. rl5's figure stands for the rl1 to rl3 lineage more than for rl5, since 58 of its 60 updates came from those runs. rl1, rl2, rl3, rl6 and rl6b can't be measured because their checkpoints are gone, and rl4 saved none, so no KL exists for any intraday run from base. No training run measured its token distance from base while it ran. The drift guards watched behaviour rates only, which is how nine NAV runs barely moved the model without anyone seeing it.

## What's different

Dates are ET.

| | NAV1-9 (Codex) | rl1-3 (Opus, Trader Training) | rl4-6b (Opus, Researcher; Fable's gates) | F, H (Opus, this session) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Dates** | 8-9 Oct | 13-18 Oct | 24-25 Oct | 2 Nov |
| **Method** | RL, `importance_sampling` | RL, `ppo` (the same with a clip on each update); rl3 adds a forecasting drill | RL, `ppo` | SFT, `cross_entropy` |
| **Reward** | Marked NAV at the horizon, one term | Force-sold at session 10, minus a SPY hedge, plus a premium on exposure, minus a risk charge and format penalties | One session, marked at the close, minus hedge and charges, minus penalties, per look | None: copy base's own text |
| **Market exposure** | Paid in full | Hedged out, then paid a small premium | Same, with nothing realized overnight | No term |
| **Open positions** | Credited at the mark, no exit cost | Sold with costs | Marked at the close less exit cost | Not applicable |
| **Doing nothing** | Cash scores 0 | No penalty | None in rl4, 0.09 R a look from rl5 | No term; every target ends in a submit |

| **Adam state** | Reset each run | Kept | Fresh | Fresh |
| **Updates behind the saved model** | NAV4 10, NAV9 16 | rl1 step 30: 30; rl3 step 8: 58 | rl5 step 2: 60, of which 2 under this reward; rl6 1; rl6b step 3: 3 | F 45, H 50 |
| **Group size** | 3 | 8 | 8 | Not applicable |

| **Effort** | Medium | Low, then high for rl3 | High | High |
| **Keep rule** | None, newest wins | Validation or pre-registered rule, rl2 excepted | Behaviour-rate guard plus Fable's gates | Last epoch by config |
| **Evaluation** | Base and parent on a few greedy cases; no market benchmark until Trader Training's re-test | Against base or the parent, paired | Against base on error rates and activity; rl5 passed a P&L non-inferiority test; beat-base and beat-cash gates never reached | Against base + free turn, 15 weeks |

**The differences that matter, most important first:**

1. **Dose in effect.** Of the models still available, NAV moved least on live looks. Its reward's effect is therefore unknown, not zero. The caution applies to Opus's intraday reward as well: rl5 had 2 updates under it, rl6 1 and rl6b 3, so its P&L effect is about as unknown as NAV's. rl5's live record is mostly the daily reward's weights.
2. **What the reward points at.** Codex's score rises with whatever the book held while prices rose. Among rollouts still holding at the end, its correlation with held position times price move is about .99 [A], and summed advantages favoured holders (+0.349) over cash (−0.165) and traders who closed (−0.185), the net coming from NAV3 and NAV4 [A]. Opus built its rewards to stop paying for that, removing the market move and charging for risk. Measured results: less exposure in rl1 (gross .98 to .62) and less research in rl2 (3.0 to .77 calls). Doing nothing out-scored trading within groups in six of eight runs (rl2, rs1 a rollouts-only sample of rl2, rl3, rl4, rl6, rl6b; t 1.6 to 2.2), not in rl1 or rl5, and held-out tests split on it.
3. **The "drift to inaction" came mostly from looks at the research limit.** In rl4 to rl6b, empty looks stayed at or under .09 on looks under the limit. In rl6 and rl6b the rise came from looks at the limit, whose share climbed from .44 to .63 and from .47 to about .7, with more of them empty; in rl4 about half did. The free turn removed the forced submit at that limit on 1 Nov, and no RL line has run since. The public README's "RL on our reward drifts toward inaction" needs this rider, and the standing case against more RL rests less on it than I assumed. What still stands against RL is that base showed no gross edge in the simulator (−$8 a week before costs, 90% −412 to +377) and a simulated path's SD is about $1,500 a week.
4. **Closeness to live hasn't bought an edge.** F and H ran the closest world and F trailed base; NAV ran the farthest and tied, on a different test. Dose and method differ too, so this doesn't show fidelity is useless.
5. **Evaluation.** Codex compared on a few greedy cases with no market benchmark and kept everything. Opus and Fable pre-registered, paired and blocked: rl4, rl6 and rl6b never deployed. That didn't produce a winner either, and rl5 went live after Clayton's rulings on results that were not shown better.

**What Codex's approach has over Opus's:** one reward term with no hand-set penalties, and horizons up to 63 sessions, closer to Clayton's days-to-three-months objective than one session. **What Opus's has over Codex's:** costs at exit, no credit for market drift, a world with live tools and shorts, and tests that can fail. Neither has been shown to beat base.

**Framework fixes changed how looks run with no training.** The free turn took empty looks from 83 to 19 of 876 and malformed looks from 8 to 1, at +$359 a week (90% −62 to +803), not shown. The parser fix cleared 103 of 122 malformed live turns.

### Two framework items for Trader Training

*Both were dealt with on the night of 2 November ET, in `agent.py` and `trader.py`; see the end of this section.*

**A closing turn that ends wrongly is thrown away and rewritten.** A turn ending in `<|return|>` (end of answer) rather than `<|call|>` (end of tool call), whether a complete submit call or orders written as the model's answer, reads as prose (`agent.py:49-58`). The framework then opens the submit call and samples again with no reasoning turn (`agent.py:124-126`). This changed 3 of the 16 decisions across both NAV sleeves on Monday:
- **NAV9, 13:00:** the plan was long XLY and CAPR, short AMZN. The rewrite placed nothing.
- **NAV9, 15:30:** the plan had trimmed sizes and put stops on new shorts. The rewrite raised two sizes, dropped those stops, and its XLP order was rejected for margin.
- **NAV9 + ML2, 13:30:** the rewrite bought 600 ULCC, which its reasoned plan had left out, and cancelled three stops. That position is −$129.

Base hit related paths too, though not this ending on Monday: Base + ML2 $100k placed nothing at 12:00 after two cut-off turns, and nothing at 14:00 after one bad-JSON turn. 3 of 16 against 0 of 42 looks that day is the one gap from base (exact p about .02, picked after looking). Stored log-probabilities don't tie it to NAV: at NAV9's two looks the tokens are about as likely under base, and at NAV9 + ML2's the answer channel is likelier under base (.97) than under NAV9 (.62). It is the same family of fault as the forced submit.

**Two exits are larger than their positions.** NAV9 + ML2 has `SELL 1200 ULCC TRAIL 8%` against a 600-share long, and `BUY 134 XLE TRAIL 5%` against a 104-share short, both placed at the 14:00 look and both still working at the close. If either fires, it reverses the position. Its reasoning added the listed fills to a position that already included them, so it may be a state-text reading problem. These are the model's orders, and software rightly carries them out.

**What Trader Training did about them.** Across all gpt-oss sleeves since 26 Oct, 8 of 338 looks hit the first path. 7 of the closing turns held the decision, and the rewrite changed 4 of those 7. Now a call ending in `<|return|>` is the call, and an answer that is the submit's JSON, give or take a short label and a fence, is the submit. Anything less clear is re-asked with a reasoning turn, and only a second such turn opens the submit.

The second item is a recurring slip: 16 of 334 gpt-oss exits on held positions were larger than the position after that submit's own entries, and in 13 the extra was exactly the fills listed, against 0 of 166 on the Claude sleeves. The state now says the positions and cash already include the listed fills. That is a fact in the state, and no order was changed. Neither fix has a measured effect yet.

## What this supports, and what it doesn't

- NAV9's tie with base neither credits nor faults the method. The tie is from NAV's simulator, and on live looks NAV9 barely differs from base.
- SFT on base's own samples moved the weights: F did worse on its test weeks, and H can't be told apart from base.
- The daily reward moved behaviour, and live pairings don't show it helped: a trained model led base in 1 of 5, across weeks with very different results.
- A NAV-type reward at a dose that moves the weights has not been run, so its effect is unknown. Nor has an intraday reward at real dose under the free turn.
- This study doesn't show that training can't beat base, or that NAV9 and base are the same weights. Token distance is not the same as outcomes.

## Limits and what I didn't check

- **Audit-only figures** [A]: every run's tokens carrying gradient, NAV1 to NAV4's training rows, the holder and cash advantage nets, the .99 correlation, $57.21 and the 15.5%. Their inputs were deleted on 14 Oct. Codex's session logs survive privately and confirm part of the record, including run details and the model.

Why repeated base scorings come back bit-identical more often than other pairs is open (prefix cache or batching); it doesn't change this reading.
- **Not settled:** whether NAV's training effort of medium matters, since no live look at medium exists. There is no same-state comparison of NAV9 and base on real decisions.
- **The check:** five readers, a comparer, a skeptic, a critic, a figure checker (about 290 items) and a cold reader, $0 and read-only, on the same model as me.
