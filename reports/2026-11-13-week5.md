## What the live test says about the models

- **Does training help? Not shown.** Without the tool, c1, F and H trailed Base $90k by 6.2, 5.9 and 2.1 points. With ML3, against Base + ML3 at $30,000, F led by 0.9 and H by 1.7, and c1 trailed by 2.0. Every pairing is confounded: the base sleeves carry the Kelly paragraph and the trained ones don't, and Base $90k is three times the size. All the $30,000 gaps are inside the noise we measured between identical runs at that size. In simulation, c1 wasn't better than base in both configurations, and F and H weren't better at all. H, the hindsight-best SFT model, was the best gpt-oss sleeve at $30,000 this week; one week doesn't make it a result.
- **Does the forecast tool help? The ML3 twin trailed in three of four pairs:** Base $90k by 7.3 points, c1 by 1.9, H by 2.3; F + ML3 led F by 0.7. Over weeks 2–5 the "+ ML" twin has led in 9 of 14 pairs, and on average in 3 of 4 calendar weeks; this week's average was −2.7 points. The pairs in a week share one market, so weeks are the units, and four weeks can't settle it. ML3 is a new version, so this week starts its own record.
- **Claude against gpt-oss.** The three Claude sleeves made +0.60% to +0.73% on 3–18 orders, mostly holding SPY and short-term Treasury ETFs. That beat eight of the ten gpt-oss sleeves and trailed Base $90k and SPY. Until the gpt-oss looks went parallel on Wednesday morning, the Claude orders waited for ten gpt-oss sleeves to finish first, about 20 minutes or more after the look time (estimated); since then they go out within about 5–6 minutes.
- **The Kelly paragraph** is on Base + ML3, the Base $90k pair and the Claude sleeves, none of which has a twin without it, so its effect isn't measured. Those sleeves ranged from −4.20% to +3.12%.
- **Against SPY, cash and the goal.** Base $90k beat SPY and the goal; nothing else did. Five sleeves beat cash.
- **Sample size.** Each sleeve's week is one draw. Between identical base runs at $30,000 we measured $1,700–2,100 a week of noise (5.7–7.0% of NAV); it has never been measured at $90,000. The gap within the Base $90k pair (7.3 points) is the largest this week, and it's one pair.

## What we learned

1. **Base gpt-oss's probabilities carry no information about which stocks beat the median.** Kelly v0 is the cleanest test of this so far: 49 windows, four draws, AUC 0.497.
2. **No new information source passed.** Six numeric inputs and one text input failed; the text lead didn't survive a second half-year. This joins F8, SD1 and the seven direction model families.
3. **A reward that can't see direction trained a model that trades like base.** c1 built base's book in the closed-loop test and lost the same on the gap. Cutting risk by imitation needs a bigger cut than one round of best-of-8 SFT gives.
4. **The overnight gap is partly forecastable in size, not enough for shock nights.** ML3's band covers about 79% of gaps out of sample once refit, but the chip gaps of 6 Nov were up to 1.8 times its top.
5. **The 15% shock term bound often.** It refused 407 orders, nearly all on the gpt-oss sleeves, which kept proposing concentrated chip positions. We haven't read whether those refusals helped or hurt.
