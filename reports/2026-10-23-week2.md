Tinker spend this week was live gpt-oss looks only, about $4 a day for four sleeves (an estimate; settled figures come from our Tinker Owner). Claude Opus at maximum effort cost about $23 a day for its two sleeves.

**rl3.** rl3 is rl2 step 10 trained for 8 more GRPO steps at high reasoning effort, with a drill that asks for calibrated fill and win probabilities ([training report](2026-10-18-training-rl1-to-rl3.md)). It passed its pre-registered simulation rule against rl2, but the whole gain came from fewer malformed answers. Live this week it lost 7.74% alone and 15.71% with the forecast tool. Alone it finished 3.1 points ahead of base; with the tool it finished 20.0 points behind base with the tool. Taken together, the two rl3 sleeves lost $7,034 and the two base sleeves lost $1,944. rl3 + forecast's largest loss was MRNA (−$3,344 on closed trades), most of it on Thursday.

**rl3's order probabilities.** The drill was meant to make rl3's probabilities useful. Live, the fill probabilities of all six sleeves beat a constant, but rl3's are the worst of the gpt-oss sleeves: it gives high probabilities (mean 0.84 and 0.93) to orders that fill 80% and 86% of the time. Win probabilities resolve five sessions after each fill and are still pending.

**Forecast rows outside the universe.** The forecast tool used to cover only 523 symbols, and names outside them came back "not covered". The models are pooled across symbols, so any US stock or ETF with about 60 sessions of IBKR bars can be scored. On a walk-forward split of 81 stocks outside the S&P, range coverage was 79% (73% under $50M daily volume) and R² 0.29 against 0.24 for the naive forecast. The split was post hoc, so the rows carry a warning. The simulator still says "not covered" for these names, so training and live now differ here.

**Bond, credit and FX ETFs.** The same split found the range forecast badly miscalibrated for Treasury, credit and currency funds: 38–40% coverage against the 80% target. The pooled model adds about 0.2 points of range from market-wide terms to every symbol, which is negligible on a stock and dominates a low-volatility bond fund. Each fund's own 20-session average range does better there. A fix will be pre-registered before it ships.

## What the live test says about the models

- **Does training help? Not yet.** rl3 beat base without the tool (−7.74% against −10.80%) and lost badly to it with the tool (−15.71% against +4.32%). Week 1's rl1 also trailed base (−4.79% against −3.86%). After two weeks no trained checkpoint has beaten the untrained model it came from.
- **Does the forecast tool help? Two pairs of three say yes this week.** Base + forecast finished 15.1 points ahead of base, Opus + forecast 11.2 points ahead of Opus, and rl3 + forecast 8.0 points behind rl3. After two days, Base + forecast's lead came mostly from two trades (MRNA and INTC), and three quarters of the Opus gap from one name outside the tool's coverage at the time (CLF, which only Opus traded). The tool's direct effect is hard to separate from luck in one week.
- **Against SPY, cash and the goal:** two sleeves of six beat all three, both with the forecast tool. Every sleeve without the tool lost more than 7%, against SPY's −0.42%.
- **Opus is no stronger than gpt-oss here.** Opus alone ranked fourth, between rl3 and base. Opus + forecast was second, behind base + forecast.

## What we learned

1. **The tool sleeves traded more calmly.** Opus + forecast placed 149 orders against Opus's 218 and paid 38% less commission; it ended the week flat. Base + forecast also ended flat. The losing sleeves held concentrated or leveraged books.
2. **Losses came from concentration and leverage, not commissions.** Commissions were $62–$153 a sleeve, against losses of $2,300–$4,700. rl3 + forecast lost $3,344 on MRNA alone. Base ended the week holding 2,866 UVXY shares on borrowed cash, about 2× its value.
3. **rl3's training target did not transfer.** The drill improved the simulator's answer format, not live probability estimates or live returns.
4. **The paper engine sometimes fills at impossible prices.** Two fills on Thursday were about +$600 better than any real fill could have been. We report results with and without them.

## What we train next

1. **Bond, credit and FX calibration of the range forecast**, pre-registered and tested out of sample before it ships.
2. **Bring the simulator up to live:** on-demand forecast rows and news articles in training, so a policy trains on the tools it uses live.
3. **Score win probabilities** once week 2's orders resolve, for all six sleeves.
4. **No new RL run until it can be judged.** The next run starts from rl3 only with an evaluation large enough to see an edge (over 1,300 decisions per arm), and goes live only if it beats base there.

## Incidents

- **A fill lost at the Gateway's midnight (22 Oct).** IB Gateway served executions only from midnight in its UTC+8 login time zone (12:00 New York time). A 120-share NVDA fill at 11:59:22 was never served, so Base + forecast's next look saw itself flat and sold another 100 shares. The fill was recorded from IBKR's trade confirmation; the model's second decision stands. With Clayton's approval the TWS Gateway Owner moved the Gateway to New York time on 23 Oct, which removes the cutoff.
- **Stalled cancels (22 Oct).** About half of the cancels after 09:40 came back pending; all took effect late and none of those orders filled. The TWS Gateway Owner's log analysis places the cause on IBKR's side, most likely the paper engine.
- **Forecast labels and sizes (20–21 Oct).** Every forecast after 12:00 New York time read "stale" because the check used the machine's UTC+8 date, and Opus + forecast's costs always assumed a $3,000 order. Both fixed on 21 Oct.
- **Held names outside the universe were shown at cost** in every sleeve's view of its book (fixed 21 Oct). The 4:15 pm records were never affected.
- **One degenerate gpt-oss answer** (Base + forecast, 23 Oct 09:30: no orders, garbled notes), 1 of 283 gpt-oss looks.
- **A macro calendar rebuild failed** on 21 Oct (a bad FRED response); a rerun succeeded and the fetch now retries.
