## What the live test says about the models

- **Does training help? Not this week.** Without the tool, Candidate 1 trailed Base $90k (B) by 1.1 points and Candidate 2 by 9.4. With ML4, Candidate 1 + ML4 trailed Base + ML4 $90k by 7.0 points and Candidate 2 + ML4 by 2.4. All four sleeves share base's text B; Candidate 2 also has the regime line. In the final-turn reads Candidate 1 trades a little less than base and Candidate 2 like base, and live both lost the comparisons with base.
- **Does the forecast tool help? The ML4 twin led in all four pairs:** base by 10.0 points, Candidate 1 by 4.1, Candidate 2 by 17.0, Fable by 5.0. In every pair the sleeve with the tool carried a more net-short book into Friday's fall: base −0.72x against −0.62x, Candidate 1 −0.69x against +0.04x, Candidate 2 −0.46x against +0.20x, Fable −0.55x against −0.39x. So the gaps are exposure on one falling day. Whether the tool caused that exposure isn't shown. Earlier verified attributions found exposure and timing behind the twin gaps, not the tool's numbers. Over weeks 2–6 the "+ ML" twin has led in 13 of 18 pairs, and on average in 4 of 5 calendar weeks. Pairs in a week share one market, so weeks are the units. ML4 is a new version, so this is its first week.
- **Text A against text B:** Base $90k (B) +5.87% against the control's +0.85%, 5.0 points to text B. It's one pair, inside the noise below.
- **The long run.** Base + ML4, four weeks without a restart, made +8.45% against the fresh Base + ML4 $90k's +15.90%. They differ in size ($30k against $90k) and in their start, so the comparison is in percent only. A study of Thursday's books found no sign of degradation over the long run, but couldn't rule it out either. Over its four weeks it made +6.37% against SPY −8.74%, short of the +8.84% goal pace. Earlier analysis put its week 2–5 results almost entirely on its SPY and SMH exposure times the market's moves, and this week, net short into a falling market, fits that.
- **Fable against gpt-oss.** On Friday the Fable sleeves held about 0.5–1.3x gross against the gpt-oss sleeves' 3x, placed 121–195 orders against 403–568, and paid about $67 in commission against $250–436. Fable 5.2 + ML4 (+3.13%) beat Base $90k (A) and Candidate 2; Fable 5.2 (−1.88%) beat only Candidate 2. In a week when SPY fell 8%, lower gross meant less gain from the short side.
- **Against SPY, cash and the goal.** All ten beat SPY, eight beat cash, seven reached the goal. That's one falling week, with net short books at about 3x.
- **Sample size.** Each sleeve's week is one draw. Between identical base runs at $30,000 and 2x gross we measured $1,700–2,100 a week of noise (5.7–7.0% of NAV). It's never been measured at $90,000 or at 3x, where it's likely wider. Five of this week's gaps are inside that range (Candidate 1 against base, Candidate 2 + ML4 against Base + ML4, the Candidate 1 and Fable tool pairs, text A against B); four are larger (Candidate 2 against base, Candidate 1 + ML4 against Base + ML4, the base and Candidate 2 tool pairs), and each is one pair in one week.

## What we learned

1. **Without a software limit, the models used far more leverage than before, and the account paid.** On Monday the shared account reached 3.45x gross ($4.79M on $1.39M), and IBKR liquidated 12 names at 15:51 ET. It liquidated 6 more on Tuesday. Once told to keep gross at or under 3x their own NAV, the gpt-oss sleeves mostly did. On Friday 1 to 5 looks a sleeve still left them a little above 3.00, at most 3.27.
2. **This week's gains are exposure in a falling market.** For seven of the eight gpt-oss sleeves the week's best day was Friday, from net-short books held at about 3x through a 3.55% SPY fall. The same exposure lost on Thursday. This repeats week 4's lesson in the other direction: the books' net direction times the market's move decides the week.
3. **Training on base's cleanest looks didn't make a better trader.** Both candidates lost to base in both comparisons. Their reads predicted "about like base", and that's what they did.
4. **A recorded decision isn't a fresh sample.** Base's recorded live decisions fault more often (29%) than base asked again on the same prompt (18–22%). Comparing a fresh candidate with recorded base had made Candidate 1 look 41% cleaner.
5. **No new direction model passed.** The ML4 direction models joined the seven earlier families; the one apparent signal was market timing, matched by a placebo.
6. **Framework rules that hold the model to its own orders need to be exact.** The close-only flag (an order marked as a close may not add to the position) dropped valid protective orders when their entry hadn't filled yet. On Friday seven sleeves had orders not sent, and some new shorts went to the close without the stop the model wrote. A fix is built and waits for a day boundary.
