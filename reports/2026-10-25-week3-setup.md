# Week 3 setup: rl4 failed, rl5 goes live (26–30 October 2026)

*Trader Training, written 25 October 2026, before week 3's first look. Week 2's results are [here](2026-10-23-week2.md).*

## What changed after week 2

Week 2 showed what the old simulator could not see. rl3 was trained on one pre-open decision whose orders then ran untouched for 10 sessions. Live, it trades every 30 minutes with a book, resting orders and notes that carry over. Its worst loss was a long of 90–93% of NAV in one name, built in one look and held with no stop through 15 looks and overnight. So this weekend's training moved to the live episode shape, and model work got its own roles:

- **Trading Model Researcher** (a Claude session): designs, pre-registers and trains the models.
- **Model Performance Analyst** (a Claude session): analysed week 2 and wrote the acceptance criteria, before any result.
- **Trader Training:** verifies each candidate against those criteria, runs the live dry run and puts approved models in the sleeves.

Clayton's rules for week 3: the RL sleeves run a new checkpoint only if it is verified by Sunday evening; otherwise they sit in cash. rl3 does not run again.

## Models trained this weekend

| Model | Started from | What it trains on | Steps and cost | Result | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **rl4** | rl3, step 8 (with its optimizer state) | Intraday simulator: four looks in one session, with a book, resting orders and notes that persist, at high effort | Stopped at step 4 by its drift guard, about $43 | Looks with no orders rose from 8.6% to 18.2%; no checkpoint saved | **Failed** |
| **rl5**, step 2 | rl3, step 8 | rl4's simulator with the reward that taught inaction removed | Stopped at step 4 by its drift guard; candidate step 2; about $37.50 training | Passed the hard acceptance criteria after one audited ruling (below) | **Live from Monday 26 Oct** |

The rl4/rl5 programme, with its evaluations, cost $149.12 of its $210 cap. The project has spent about $300 of its $400 Tinker budget.

**The intraday simulator.** An episode starts from an inherited book: a random loss of −2% to +15% since the $30,000 start, and one to five cheap pre-open decisions by the current policy. Then come four looks on the same session, at 09:00, 10:30, 13:00 and 15:30 New York time. Each uses the live prompt, the live state renderer and the live tools (quotes, bars, news, articles, tape, stats, scanner, and the forecast in half the groups), all answering as of the look time from 30-minute bars. Training sessions run from 20 October 2025 to 30 June 2026; everything after June 2026, and the week-2 live looks, are held out. The reward marks positions at the close and carries them, with forward days, to the Monday reset. It charges concentration, idiosyncratic risk and unprotected overnight carry, and penalises rejected orders, malformed answers and orders placed without research.

**Why rl4 failed.** Measured within groups, a look with no orders earned 0.186 R more reward than an active look at the same point. Most of that (0.124 R) was shaping penalties that only an active look could pay: unresearched orders, small entries, rejects. GRPO learned "don't act" as well as "act cleanly". The drift guard stopped the run at step 4, before its first checkpoint save.

**What rl5 changed.** An empty look now pays 0.12 R, the average penalty an active look paid. Token actions count as empty: a few shares, limits far from the market, or cancelling and replacing far orders. The small-entry penalty was dropped. The unresearched penalty now applies only to orders that add exposure. Re-scoring rl4's 1,536 stored looks offline with rl5's terms cut the empty-versus-active gap from +0.186 R to +0.011 R (SE 0.10) before any money was spent. rl5 still drifted: its guard stopped it at step 4 on the inactive share at the 10:30 look (8.3% to 19.8%), so the pre-registered candidate is step 2.

## How rl5 was judged

All criteria were written before rl5's results. Each is against the untrained base model.

| Test | What it measures | rl5 step 2 | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Replays week 2's live looks: misreads, rule errors, activity | Passes the error and activity floors. The side-misread metric (M3) failed on its scorer's count (+0.0082 against a +0.0041 bound) | Pass after a blind audit (below) |
| R2a | rl3's daily simulator, July–October 2026 | −0.136 R raw, −0.191 R winsorized, against a −0.5 R bound | Pass |
| R4 | Answer quality | Malformed 0% (base 2.7%); forced-empty looks 11.3% (base 17.1%); notes 99% | Pass |
| R2b | Intraday simulator P&L, July–October 2026 (reported only) | +0.20 R against base, better on 17 of 24 dates | Reported |
| R3 | Positions carried overnight without a protective order (reported only) | 98% against base's 74% | Reported, a watch item |
| R8 | Live dry run through the real loop, nothing placed | Every submit parsed, probabilities on every order, 62–134 s a look | Pass |

**The M3 audit.** M3's automatic scorer flags orders whose side contradicts the model's own notes. A blind audit of its 28 flags found rl5 with 0 true misreads of 12 (2 arguable) and base with 2 of 16, a scorer precision of 2 in 28. Clayton ruled that M3 is judged on the audited count, and that rl5 strictly failing its probability format on 3 of 2,616 orders is immaterial. rl5 step 2 was approved for the RL sleeves.

**What to watch live.** rl5 leaves more positions unprotected overnight than base, places more orders under $1,000 (0.73 to 1.17 token orders a look in training), and was still drifting toward inaction when stopped. None of these is a hard criterion; all are counted each day of week 3.

## What the models see from Monday

Software still never blocks or changes a decision. Its presentation changed for all six sleeves alike, so week 3 compares like with like:
- a buying-power line, and LONG or SHORT spelled out for every position;
- "cancel pending" on orders whose cancel is not yet confirmed;
- labelled quote fields with the change from the previous close;
- the forecast's next-session range shown as a ± band, with no direction;
- a dollars-at-risk line per position, from its 20-session volatility;
- a pace line in place of the weekly dollar goal: "Sleeve now $X (+y% since its $30,000 start)", against the +200% a year target;
- the Monday reset stated in the sleeve line.

Clayton kept the sleeve terms as they were: 2× gross exposure checked at order time, and every position closed at Monday's open. They rejected a prompt sentence about concentration and any required stop or single-name cap.
