**rl5 live.** rl5 traded every day and stayed active: 4 of its 28 looks on Monday and Tuesday placed no substantive order (base: 3). The weaknesses training showed came through live. On Monday and Tuesday it had a protective order on 3% of held positions against base's 13%, entered without a stop 55 times in 57 (base 50 in 71), and put 15 orders under $1,000 (base 35). It ran its book near the 2× limit and was rejected twice there. Its forecast twin did much better (+7.09%), with fewer unresearched entries (2 against 5 on those two days).

**rl6 and rl6b.** Both ask whether rl5's reward, applied to base instead of the rl3 lineage, removes the errors without inheriting that lineage's style of no stops, small lots and many names. At 2e-4 the policy drifted within one update, first to token orders, then to inaction. At 5e-5 three updates gave a near-base checkpoint: unprotected promised stops 8.2% against base's 8.8%, unresearched entries 5.3% against 4.3%. The acceptance bar, which Clayton set higher than rl5's, requires a margin of no more than 2 points on every error type; an equal model passes all of them only about 1% of the time, so a near-base checkpoint correctly fails. Neither joined week 3. The same evaluation re-scored rl5 on this week's forecast text: it fails the unresearched-entries test (+5.5 points) and puts 15.3% of its orders under $1,000, which made both its live watch items.

**F8.** Every RL run so far has drifted toward doing nothing, and the reading of all three sessions is that the simulator gives a policy no information with a tested edge, so the reward's trading charges make inaction cheaper than acting. F8 tested the most plausible missing signal, earnings drift, before spending anything on training with it. It shows no gross drift at all, let alone one that beats costs. No further paid RL aimed at P&L is planned until a new information source passes a test like this.

## What the live test says about the models

- **Does training help? Not this week.** rl5 trailed base by 7.2 points alone (+0.63% against +7.83%) and by 5.1 points with the tool (+7.09% against +12.20%). Over three weeks, our trained checkpoint has trailed base in four of five pairings (week 1 rl1 −0.9 points; week 2 rl3 +3.1 and −20.0; week 3 rl5 −7.2 and −5.1).
- **Does the forecast tool help? Yes, in all three pairs this week:** base +4.4 points, rl5 +6.5, Opus +5.1. Over weeks 2 and 3 the "+ forecast" sleeve beat its twin in five of six pairs. The tool offers no direction forecast, only risk and cost; the twins' gap is therefore about sizing and selection, not prediction.
- **Against SPY, cash and the goal.** Five sleeves beat SPY and cash; four beat the goal (base, base + forecast, rl5 + forecast, Opus + forecast). Opus alone lost 1.68%, with the fewest fills (120), most of the loss on Thursday and Friday.
- **Sample size.** Each sleeve's week is one draw. Week 2's daily series implied weekly swings of about 2–11% per sleeve, and the gpt-oss sleeves held similar defensive books this week, so their gains are largely one shared position and not four independent results.

## What we learned

1. **The untrained base model was the best gpt-oss trader again.** Base + forecast has now beaten SPY, cash and the goal two weeks running (+4.32%, then +12.20%).
2. **RL on this reward drifts toward inaction from either starting point.** rl4, rl5, rl6 and rl6b all hit their inaction or token-order guards within four updates. Lowering the learning rate only delays it.
3. **The direction information we have is not an edge.** Seven direction model families and now an earnings-drift rule all fail after costs. Without such a signal, P&L-based acceptance tests (beat base, beat cash) cannot be passed by training on the same information.
4. **The forecast tool's risk numbers help every model.** Five of six twin pairs over two weeks favour the tool.
5. **rl5's simulated advantage did not carry to live.** It passed its acceptance criteria and was +0.20 R against base in the intraday simulator; live it trailed base in both pairs with the weaknesses the simulator flagged.

## What we train next

1. **No paid P&L-aimed RL until a new information source passes a pre-registered test like F8.** Candidates are proposed and tested offline at no Tinker cost first.
2. **Keep the live test running unchanged at the 2 Nov reset** (base, rl5 and Opus, each with and without the tool), so the training question gets more draws: one week of 70 looks cannot detect the edges we look for, which need more than 1,300 decisions per model.
3. **Study why base + forecast does well**, from its looks: what it reads in the tool and how it sizes, as the basis for any next reward.
4. **Fix the reward's pull toward inaction** before any further run from base, if Clayton approves another attempt; the data points at the trading charges, not the learning rate.
